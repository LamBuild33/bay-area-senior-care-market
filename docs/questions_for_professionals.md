# Questions for my accountant, lawyer and insurer

October 5, 2026

These are the questions to settle with an accountant, a lawyer and an insurance agent before my first paid client. They are questions to bring, not legal or tax advice.

## Accountant

An accountant answers the tax and money-handling questions. **Bring:** the agreement draft (sections 5 to 8 and 11), a sample weekly invoice, and a rough idea of how many hours a week you expect to work.

1. **Contractor or household employee, for tax purposes.** With one regular client, am I a self-employed business or a household employee? What do the client and I each owe in taxes? *Why I'm asking:* the agreement says I run my own business, but one regular client may change how the tax rules see it. The same question goes to the lawyer.
2. **California household employer payroll.** California has a quarterly wage amount that makes a household an employer who must run payroll. What is the current figure, and could a client cross it by paying me? *Why I'm asking:* I could not confirm the number from public pages, and a client who crosses it would have paperwork to do.
3. **The 4-hour client and the Care.com client.** Does it change anything if one client is a 4-hour-a-week client, or if I find a client through Care.com? *Why I'm asking:* these are the first clients I expect, and I want to know the tax answer before I sign anyone.
4. **Taxes I pay as self-employed.** What do I set aside, how often do I pay (quarterly estimated payments), and what do I need to register or file at the start? *Why I'm asking:* so I do not get a surprise bill and know which forms come first.
5. **Mileage.** I charge clients the IRS standard mileage rate for drives with or for them (76 cents a mile for July to December 2026), and my own drive to the client's home is free. How do I record that mileage, and how is the mileage money treated on my taxes? *Why I'm asking:* the money is a charge to the client, and I want it recorded correctly.
6. **Purchases and reimbursements.** I use the client's card for purchases, and my own card only as a backup under $100, which the client repays within 24 hours. How do I record those repayments so they are not counted as my income? *Why I'm asking:* section 7 of the agreement sets this up, and I want my records to match it.
7. **Cash, checks and Zelle.** What records do I keep for each way a client can pay, and how long do I keep tax records? *Why I'm asking:* section 8 lets clients pay by check, cash or Zelle, and I give a receipt for every cash payment.

## Lawyer

The lawyer answers what the law allows and how the agreement is worded. **Bring:** the agreement draft with its drafting notes (the last section of the draft), the medication fact sheet from California's Home Care Services Bureau once you have read it, and the Care.com terms once you have read them. Section numbers below match the agreement.

1. **Contractor or household employee (section 11).** With one regular client, a 4-hour client, or a client found through Care.com, am I an independent business or a household employee? *Why I'm asking:* section 11 only states our intent, and the law may decide differently.
2. **Home care licensing.** Does California home care licensing apply to one person working alone? Does it matter that I find clients through Care.com? Is the wording "Registered Home Care Aide" in sections 1 to 3 safe to use? *Why I'm asking:* I need to know whether I may work this way before I start.
3. **Medication (section 3).** I only remind, remind about refills, open containers and pick up prescriptions. Does that match what the state fact sheet allows an aide to do? *Why I'm asking:* I have only seen the fact sheet quoted, and I want the wording confirmed before a client relies on it.
4. **Blood pressure and heart rate (section 3).** May an aide take and record these? The draft has a bracketed option where the client uses their own device and I only write down the numbers. *Why I'm asking:* this is a common request, but the line between recording and nursing is unclear to me.
5. **Meal and routine help (sections 2 and 4).** Is "help you plan meals and daily routines from public guidance and your doctors' instructions" safe, or should it be narrower? *Why I'm asking:* "dietitian" is a protected title and individual medical nutrition advice is regulated, so I want to know where planning ends and advice begins.
6. **Hours and overtime (section 5).** I work up to 12 hours a day. Does a day that long raise overtime or employee questions? *Why I'm asking:* a long day is allowed in the agreement, and I want to be sure it does not change my status.
7. **Reporting abuse (section 12).** Am I required to report suspected elder abuse? To whom, and how should the agreement word it? *Why I'm asking:* I assumed I must, and I want the duty and the wording right.
8. **Health privacy and records (section 10).** How do I handle health details in visit notes, who may see them, and is one year enough to keep records before I delete or shred them? *Why I'm asking:* the notes contain private health information.
9. **Late cancellation (section 9).** A client who cancels with under 24 hours' notice pays the shortest-visit time at their hourly rate, and nothing for a medical emergency or hospital stay. Is that charge acceptable? *Why I'm asking:* I want to know the charge can be enforced and is fair.
10. **Ending or pausing care (sections 8 and 14).** Can I pause visits when an invoice is 7 days late, and end the agreement with 30 days' notice, without legal trouble for leaving a client who depends on me? *Why I'm asking:* these are the two ways I stop work, and I want them to be safe.
11. **Wills, joint accounts and power of attorney (sections 7 and 13).** Should the agreement add a clause on these? *Why I'm asking:* older clients sometimes offer gifts or add me to accounts, and I want a firm rule.
12. **Disputes and governing law (section 14).** What should the agreement say about settling disagreements? *Why I'm asking:* I left this blank on purpose for you to decide.
13. **Care.com and agency terms.** If my first client comes through Care.com or an agency, do its terms set my pay or conditions? Care.com's terms may ban using its messages to market a business, send disputes to private arbitration, and screen people in my household. *Why I'm asking:* a platform that sets my pay or terms can make me look like an employee, and I have not verified these points in the terms myself.
14. **Paid driving.** I drive clients in my own car and charge mileage. Do the state's rules for paid passenger carriers apply to an aide? *Why I'm asking:* I could not settle this from public pages. The same question goes to my car insurer.

## Insurance

Two people answer these: an insurance broker (who compares business policies) and your car insurer (who covers your car). **Bring:** a plain description of the work (non-medical help at home, errands, driving clients, one person working alone) and the services list from section 2 of the agreement.

**Insurance broker**

1. **Business coverage.** What general liability, bonding and accident coverage fits a one-person, non-medical home support business? What would quotes cost? *Why I'm asking:* general liability pays if I damage a client's property or a client is hurt because of me; bonding covers theft; I need to know which ones matter for this work.
2. **What goes in section 13.** Which insurers and policy limits (the most a policy pays) can I name in the agreement, and can I get a certificate of insurance to show a client? *Why I'm asking:* section 13 has a blank for insurer names and limits, and it says I carry general liability and professional liability but not a bond.
3. **Cases that worry me.** Is a client who falls while I help them covered? Is a claim that something was stolen covered? What about my own injury on the job? *Why I'm asking:* these are the most likely problems in home care, and I want to know what is and is not paid.

**Car insurer**

4. **Driving clients.** Does my policy cover driving clients, and driving for errands for them, when I charge mileage and my hourly rate? Please confirm in writing. *Why I'm asking:* my personal policy may exclude driving for pay, and I could not settle this from public pages.
5. **If my policy says no.** What would it cost to add coverage for this, and is there a different kind of policy I should buy? *Why I'm asking:* I should not drive a client until I know the answer.
6. **Passenger limits.** Does it matter that I never carry anyone who needs a wheelchair van or stretcher? *Why I'm asking:* section 6 of the agreement says driving is a favor of convenience, not medical transport, and I want the insurer to agree with that line.
