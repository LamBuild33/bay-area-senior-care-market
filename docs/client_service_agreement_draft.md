# Client Service Agreement (draft) - [Last Name] Home Support

October 5, 2026

## 1. Who this is between and what it covers

This agreement is between **[Client name]** ("you") and **[Your full name]**, operating as **[Last name] Home Support** ("I"). It covers the non-medical help at home described below, starting **[start date]**. I am a Registered Home Care Aide.

If a family member or other person arranges or pays for your care, they are named here: **[Name and relationship, or "none"]**. They may schedule visits, receive visit notes if you agree in section 10, and pay my invoices.

## 2. Services I provide

I help you stay comfortable and independent at home. Services we agree on, checked on the intake sheet:

- Companionship, conversation and planned activities such as walks, games, reading and reminiscing
- Meal planning, shopping, cooking and clean-up
- Light housekeeping and laundry
- Help with dressing, grooming and moving around the home
- Errands and appointments, including driving you in my car (section 6)
- Organizing mail, files, forms, calendars and appointments (section 3 sets the limits)
- Help with phones, computers and other technology
- Following exercise, meal or activity routines that your own doctor or therapist has set (section 4)
- Organizing closets, cabinets and rooms, and checking food for expiration dates and throwing out expired items, with your permission
- Helping you plan meals and daily routines, using general public health guidance and your own doctors' instructions. You decide what you want to do (section 4)

On the first visit we fill out an intake sheet. It records your own goals in your words, what you can already do, and what you would like to do more of on your own. I use it to plan each visit and to check progress with you.

## 3. What I do not do

My work is non-medical. These limits protect your safety and keep me within what a Registered Home Care Aide may do.

**Medication.** I may remind you to take your medication, remind you to refill it, open containers you cannot open, and pick up prescriptions as an errand. I do not give or hand-feed medication, sort or set up pills, give injections or patches, or decide whether you should take a dose.

**Medical care.** I do not do nursing tasks such as wound care, ostomy care, dialysis help or checking blood sugar. I do not diagnose, treat or give medical advice. **[Blood pressure and heart rate: if you want this, you use your own home device at your direction and I write down the numbers you read, without interpreting them or changing anything because of them. Confirm with a lawyer and the state fact sheet before keeping this sentence.]** If something looks wrong, I tell you and your family or call for help (section 12).

**Paperwork and money.** I organize only: sorting mail, filing, filling out forms with you, making calls with you, and keeping appointments and calendars. I do not use your logins or passwords, sign or write checks, pay bills, or handle your bank accounts. If you need bill paying, I can point you to a daily money manager.

**Other.** I do not give legal or financial advice, and I do not take on tasks I am not trained or allowed to do. I will tell you when a request is outside this list.

## 4. Following your doctors' and therapists' plans

I support your recovery and independence. I do not provide therapy, diagnose or assess your condition, write medical or therapeutic diet plans, or promise a medical result. I can help you plan meals and daily routines using general, widely published health guidance and what your own doctors have told you. You decide what you want to do, and I do not call any of it treatment.

If your doctor, physical therapist, occupational therapist, dietitian or other professional gives you an exercise, meal or activity plan, I can help you follow it exactly as written: reminders, standing nearby, cooking to their instructions, and noting what you did. You or your family give me a copy of the plan, and I keep it with your records. If a plan changes, I use the new one only once I have it in writing. If I think a plan is not working or you are having trouble with it, I tell you and your family so you can ask the professional. I do not change it myself.

## 5. Visits, rates and minimums

Your rate is **$[35 or 40] an hour**, and the shortest visit I schedule for you is **[2 or 4] hours**. These depend on the drive time from my base to your home each way, which is about **[number] minutes**.

- I bill for the actual time I work, from arrival to departure, at your hourly rate divided by the time, rounded to the nearest **minute**. The shortest-visit minimum above still applies.
- I work up to 12 hours in a day.
- Visits are one or more a week, on days and times we agree in writing (text or email is fine). Our starting schedule is **[days, times, hours]**.
- I do not take clients more than 45 minutes away.

## 6. Driving and mileage

When you ask me to drive you, or to drive for an errand for you, I use my own car, my regular driver's license and my own car insurance. I do not charge for my own drive to and from your home. For drives with you or for you, I charge the IRS standard mileage rate in effect that day (76 cents a mile for July to December 2026), plus my hourly rate for the time.

Driving is a favor of convenience, not a medical transport service. I do not transport anyone who needs a wheelchair van, a stretcher or medical monitoring on the way. I may decline a drive in unsafe weather or when I believe it is unsafe for either of us, and I will tell you why.

## 7. Purchases made for you

When I buy groceries or other items for you, I use **your** card or payment method, with your permission each time. I keep receipts and give them to you.

I may use my own card as a backup, only for a purchase under $100, and you reimburse me within 24 hours of receiving the receipt.

I do not hold your cash or cards between visits.

## 8. Payment

I send you a written invoice **once a week** showing the date, hours, hourly rate, mileage and any purchases, with receipts attached or copied for purchases. Payment is due **within 7 days**. You can pay by check, cash or Zelle. I give a receipt for every cash payment and for every reimbursement.

If an invoice is more than **7 days** late, I may pause visits until it is paid. I do not charge late fees.

## 9. Cancellation, illness and time off

**If you cancel.** Please give me at least 24 hours' notice. If you cancel with less, you pay the shortest-visit time in section 5 at your hourly rate. There is no charge when a visit is cancelled because of a medical emergency or a hospital stay.

**If I cannot come.** I work alone and do not send a substitute. If I am sick, I tell you as early as I can, by text or call, and we reschedule. For planned time off I give you at least 14 days' notice. You pay nothing for a visit I cannot make.

## 10. Visit notes, check-ins and privacy

**Visit notes.** After each visit I write a short note: what we did, what you managed on your own, and anything I noticed. I give it to you. Family members or others you choose may also receive it, but only with your consent, which you give below and may withdraw at any time. What you tell me is confidential. I share it only with the people you name, or as section 12 allows or the law requires.

People you consent to receive notes (initial each): **[names and contact, or "only me"]**.

**Check-ins.** About every **3** months, I talk with you, and with your family if you choose, about what is working, what is not, what you can now do on your own, and what to change in the visit routine. I write a few lines afterward and give you a copy. These check-ins are also the time to talk about what could be improved and whether this arrangement is the right fit for both of us. Either of us may ask for a check-in sooner at any time.

**Your records.** I keep your intake sheet, any plans from your professionals, visit notes and check-in notes together in one place for you, and I protect them as private health information. At the end of our arrangement I give you a copy on request, and I keep or delete the rest as **keep them for one year in case we work together again, then delete or shred them [lawyer to confirm one year is long enough]**.

## 11. Independent business relationship

We both intend that I run my own business and am not your employee. In practice that means:

- I decide how I do the work, within the services in this agreement. You tell me what you need, not how to do it.
- We agree on visit times together. I set my own rates and policies.
- I use my own car, supplies and insurance.
- I may have other clients, and I may take on more over time.
- You do not pay payroll taxes or provide benefits for me. I am responsible for my own taxes.

This section states our intent. If a tax or labor agency treats the arrangement differently, we will each follow what the law requires and talk about how to adjust.

## 12. Safety and emergencies

In a medical emergency, I call 911 first. Then I call your emergency contact: **[name and phone]**. I may share what emergency responders need to help you, such as your medications and conditions, from the information you give me.

I may end a visit and leave if I feel unsafe or am asked to do something outside section 3, and I will tell you why afterward. I report to the proper authorities if I believe you are being harmed or are at risk of harm, as the law requires.

Please tell me about any hazards in the home (pets, stairs, firearms, smoking) and any health conditions I should know about before the first visit.

## 13. Insurance and gifts

**Insurance.** I carry general liability and professional liability insurance for my work, and I use my own car insurance when I drive. I do not carry a bond. **[Add insurer names and policy limits once purchased, or leave out.]**

**Gifts.** I accept small gifts, up to $100 in value each. I do not accept anything worth more.

## 14. Ending the agreement and general terms

Either of us may end this agreement at any time by telling the other, in writing (text or email counts). Please give **30** days' notice when you can. You pay for visits already made and any charges owed under section 9. I help hand over your records and plans if you are moving to another caregiver.

This agreement is our full understanding. Changes must be in writing and agreed by both of us. If part of it is found unenforceable, the rest still applies. It is governed by California law. **[Dispute resolution: decide with your lawyer.]**
